# Taming AI: Proposal Review and Activity Ideas

A review of `proposal/workshop-proposal.docx` (as it stood before the review's fixes were applied) against the three course documents in `docs/guidelines/` (the Philippines project expectations, the proposal template and the assessment rubric). It looks at the proposal from each member's discipline, from the learners' side and from the grader's side. Section 6 sets out how the Day 4 AI web game would run. Section 7 is a bank of kid-friendly activities that fit the rule that **only free AI is used during teaching**.

**What this review assumes (from the team):**
- Each session (AM / PM) has **10 learners or fewer**.
- The brief's "interactive educational AI game" means a **web game** that learners build with AI.
- Free Chinese AI (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi and others) is allowed and recommended alongside free ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude.
- Members' majors are an **optional** source of examples, not something every lesson has to use.

Labels used below:

- **Required:** the brief, template or rubric asks for it explicitly.
- **Risk:** something that could break on the day.
- **Suggestion:** my own idea, not something the course asks for.

## 3. The free-AI rule: what it changes

**The rule:** during teaching, both teachers and kids may only use free AI. **Preparing** materials beforehand is not restricted. That means bias image sets, sample products, backup images, the sample web game and videos can be made with any tool.

### 3.1 Which free tool for which job

Using Chinese AI alongside ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude is recommended. It also gives you a backup when one service is busy.

| Job in class | First choice (free) | Backups (free) |
|---|---|---|
| Explanations, simplifying words, quiz questions, story text, essay polishing | DeepSeek | Free ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Qwen, Kimi |
| **Building the HTML web game** | **DeepSeek**: the chat reportedly shows a "Run HTML" button that previews the game. Check this in the test run. | Gemini (Canvas preview), Claude (artifact preview), Qwen. Last resort: save the code as `.html` and open it in a browser. |
| Flashcard / storybook / game pictures | Free Gemini | Qwen Chat (reportedly free image generation), free Canva AI, free Copilot |
| Layout and voice recording | Free Canva (templates, recording yourself) | n/a |
| Machine learning | Teachable Machine (free, no account) | n/a |

**Accounts.** All three of these chatbots set age limits:

- ChatGPT: 13 and over.
- Gemini: under-13s only through Family Link.
- DeepSeek: says it is "primarily intended for adults".

The proposal's rule that only facilitators sign in is the right one. It should cite these terms as the reason.

**Don't use Grok with the kids (Risk).** In January 2026, Common Sense Media rated it unsafe for children and teens after its image tool generated sexualized images. Its image generation was also moved to paid subscribers, so it isn't free anyway.

**Privacy note (Suggestion):** most of these services store prompts on their servers. DeepSeek's policy says its servers are in China, which makes it a concrete example for the Day 2 privacy lesson. The safe rule is the same for every chatbot: no names, faces or school details in prompts. Keep questions non-political, because free chatbots differ in what they will answer.

### 3.2 Image quota

With 10 or fewer learners there is no need to change the plan now. Just keep it in mind.

Free image limits are small and change often. The numbers below are unofficial third-party reports from 2026:

- **Free Gemini:** about 20 images a day per account.
- **Free ChatGPT:** about 2–3.
- **Gemini's reset:** reported as midnight Pacific time, which is about 4 pm in Manila. So the AM session and most of the PM session share one quota.

**Supply and demand at this size.** Two groups need about 12 images each for flashcards (Day 2) and about 12 each for the storybook (Day 3). Across AM and PM that is about 50 images a day, against roughly 80 from 4 facilitator accounts, plus Qwen and Canva as backups.

**When to look at this again:**
- the class grows,
- groups get smaller (more groups means more images), or
- the test run shows lower limits.

**Kahoot:** the free plan is reported to allow about 10 players per game, which fits this class. If an 11th kid joins, switch to team mode.

## 4. Review by discipline

> **Guardrail.** The majors are a **lens for reviewing**, not a quota for content. The brief and the six objectives decide what is taught. A major-flavoured example earns its place only if it teaches an objective **better** than a generic example would. If it doesn't, leave it out. The "How our majors shape the workshop" table should describe what the plan really does, not drive it. The ideas below marked *optional* are fine to drop.

### 4.1 Billy: Computer Science (AI accuracy, tools, prompting)

**Strengths**
- The Teachable Machine "bad data" retrain is the right way to show how AI learns.
- The Prompt Recipe is concrete and easy for kids to remember.
- Planting a hallucination ("Mount Apo tallest in the world") is a good fact-check exercise.

**Issues and fixes**

- **Definition.** "AI is a program that learns from examples" is fine for kids, but some AI follows hand-written rules. Keep the definition, and avoid borderline items in Is it AI? (spell-check, "smart" washing machines), or label them "it depends!" as discussion cards. Suggestion.
- **Character consistency.** A text-only "character recipe" does **not** keep a character looking the same across 8–10 pictures. Generate the character once, then ask for each page **in the same chat, using that picture as the reference**. Treat any drift that remains as a "spot the AI mistake" moment. Risk.
- **Web game reliability.** AI-written games often come out with bugs: a score that doesn't count, or a level that never ends. Don't hide this. **Finding and fixing bugs by prompting** is the "AI is not always correct" lesson in action. Prepare a tested Game Prompt Recipe and a working fallback game (section 6). Risk.
- **Energy claims.** Per-prompt energy figures vary a lot between sources. Use a relative comparison ("one AI picture uses much more power than one search") rather than exact watt numbers, unless you cite a source. Suggestion.
- **AI mistakes.** Planted mistakes are reliable. Real ones are more convincing: free chatbots often get details of local legends wrong, so have UP partners check real answers, with the planted sheet as backup. Suggestion.
- **Offline backup.** There is a backup for Teachable Machine only. Days 2–4 also need a USB with pre-made images, the sample and fallback web games, offline templates, and a pocket Wi-Fi. Risk.

### 4.2 Yelina: Supply Chain Management and Analytics (logistics, data, sustainability)

**Strengths**
- Part 3 has clear owners and deadlines.
- The equipment plan is scheduled.
- Daily debriefs are planned.

**Issues and fixes**

- **The majors table overpromises.** It says Yelina brings "AI predicting demand and planning deliveries", but no lesson teaches it. Either drop that wording or use *Sari-sari Store Predictor* (section 7, optional). That activity is justified by objectives A and C, not by her major: it shows a local AI use and what goes wrong when data is missing. Suggestion.
- **AM→PM changeover.** Nothing in the schedule covers restocking or reset between sessions. Every consumable is needed ×2. With 10 or fewer kids the quantities are small, but list them. Each group's files (flashcards, storybook, `.html` game) need saving per session, with clear names such as `AM-Group1-game.html`. Risk.
- **No printing plan.** Flashcards and storybooks need printing before Day 5, or show them on laptops instead. Web games need no printing. Risk.
- **Data loop.** Use the AM exit quiz to adjust the PM session: re-teach anything fewer than 7 in 10 kids got right. This makes the "reading daily quiz results" claim real. Suggestion.
- **Risk register.** List these problems with a backup for each: Wi-Fi down, the chatbot is busy, laptop dies, homework not done, a kid absent. Suggestion.
- **Sustainability is only a 2-minute talk.** Required: the brief lists it, so it needs more than a mention. Suggestion for how: *Energy Tokens* (section 7).

### 4.3 Payton: Radiography (human checking, health data, safety)

**Strengths**
- "AI helps read X-rays, but trained people check" is accurate and a good real-world Guide example. It's a one-line example, which is the right weight for it.
- The point that biased medical data leads to unfair results is also accurate.

**Issues and fixes**

- **Consent for kids' data.** The workshop collects photos and data from minors: webcam images (hands only, which is good), voice recordings, Day 5 photos, and essays. The Philippines Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) applies. Get consent forms through the school, avoid faces in training data, and say where recordings are stored and when they are deleted. Teaching point: Teachable Machine trains **inside the browser**, so images aren't uploaded unless you save the project. Risk.
- **Red and green cards.** About 1 in 12 boys is red-green colour-blind. Use ✓ / ✗ shapes or thumbs up / down instead. Risk.
- **Narration.** Some kids are shy about recording their voice. Offer choices: narrate, do sound effects, be the "director", or record in pairs. Suggestion.
- **Cultural accuracy.** Free AI can get Philippine legends and heroes wrong. Make the UP partner check a named step in Day 3, not optional. Suggestion.

### 4.4 Raiyan: Mechanical Engineering (design process, building, testing)

**Strengths**
- PLAN → BUILD → TEST → IMPROVE is applied to every product.
- It is generic good design practice, not just an engineering flavour, so it belongs in the plan whatever anyone's major is.
- The Day 4 playtest swap with "2 Stars and a Wish" is a real test-and-improve step.

**Issues and fixes**

- **Flashcards skip TEST.** Add 5 min where groups swap decks and check "Can a Grade 2 kid read it?" Suggestion.
- **The web game still needs a paper PLAN.** Kids sketch the start screen, rules, 3 levels and scoring on paper before touching AI. That keeps the ideas theirs, and gives them something to check the AI's game against. Required by the theme ("AI helps, it doesn't do all the thinking").
- **Levels must actually get harder.** Give the Game Plan sheet a level rule: L1 = recall, L2 = example, L3 = "what would you do?" ethics question. Suggestion.

### 4.5 The kids' view (P5–P6, ages about 10–12)

**What works:**
- cute characters and local animals
- movement games
- making real things
- certificates
- a small class, so every kid gets attention and gets to speak

The web game adds the "I made a real game!" moment, which is probably the week's biggest motivator.

**What could still lose them:**

- **Waiting for the laptop.** With 10 kids in 2 groups, it's still 5 kids per laptop. Either give each pair a laptop for hands-on parts, or rotate light role cards (Prompt Chef, Fact Checker, Tester, Reporter). Suggestion.
- **Day 1 is dense.** It has 8 activities, and 4 of them are new concepts. Keep each teacher talk to **5 min or less**, then hands-on.
- **Reading level.** P5–P6 kids write for younger readers but don't know what "simple" means. Give a rule: **12 words or fewer per card back; one idea per page.**
- **Language.** Put instruction cards in English + Filipino. Suggestion: ask the same question to a chatbot in English and Filipino, and compare. It's an easy-to-grasp fairness example (AI is often better in English).

### 4.6 The assessor's view (course grading)

- **Proposal (10%).** It sits in the A range on detail and flow. The rubric gaps are videos, the game format, evaluation of learning, and Day 3 / essay feasibility.
- **Teaching materials (15%).** Add the sample storybook, the sample web game, worksheets that assess understanding, and bilingual cards.
- **Service performance (40%, individual).** Each member needs visible time leading activities. The current lead minutes are Yelina 235, Raiyan 205, Billy 205, Payton 175. That's balanced. There's no need to match activities to majors to keep it that way.
- **Reflective journal (30%, individual).** The daily debriefs in Part 3 are good material for the journal. Keep short notes each day.

## 6. Day 4: the AI-built web game

**What it teaches.** It covers the brief's skills in one task:
- writing and improving prompts,
- checking AI output (bugs and wrong answers),
- creating a learning material for others,
- seeing what still belongs to the kids (their design).

**1. Warm-up (15 min).** Play a sample single-file web game, for example Billy's *Math Meteor Defense*, on the projector. Ask the kids to name its rules, levels and score. Then play 5 min of Blob Opera: "AI can make music too."

**2. Game Plan on paper (25 min).** Each group fills in a Game Plan sheet:
- **Topic:** math, science or AI safety.
- **Game type:** a quiz race, catch-the-right-answer, or a choose-your-path.
- **Rules:** one sentence.
- **3 levels:** easy → hard.
- **Scoring:** how points are won.
- **Start screen:** a sketch.
- **Questions:** 5 written by the kids, with answers.

**3. Build with AI (30 min).** The Prompt Chef fills in the blanks of the **Game Prompt Recipe** and pastes it into DeepSeek. Press *Run HTML*, then play. Make 2–3 improvement prompts ("Level 2 is too easy", "show the score bigger"). The AI may add 5 more questions, and the Fact Checker checks every answer. The facilitator saves the `.html` file.

> **Game Prompt Recipe** (a draft for the team to test, not final wording)
> Make a complete educational game in **one HTML file** for children aged 10–12.
> Topic: ___. Game type: ___. Rules: ___.
> Level 1: ___. Level 2: ___. Level 3: ___. Scoring: ___.
> Show a start screen with the title "___" and the rules, a score on screen, and a "You win!" screen.
> Use these questions and answers exactly: ___.
> Big buttons, large friendly text, bright colours. Do not use any links, images, sounds or files from the internet.

**4. Playtest, Bug Hunt and Improve (25 min).** Groups play each other's games. Testers fill a "2 Stars, a Wish and a Bug" card. Each group fixes at least one bug or wish by prompting, then saves the final version.

**Evaluation plan:**
- Each game has a rules screen, 3 levels, a visible score and at least 10 checked questions.
- Each group found and fixed at least one AI bug by prompting.
- Each kid can name one thing they designed and one thing the AI made.

**Risks and fallbacks:**
- **The DeepSeek preview button is missing, or the service is busy.** Use Gemini Canvas, Claude or Qwen. As a last resort, paste the code into a `.html` file and open it.
- **The game is badly broken.** Use a **fallback template game** made beforehand with any tool. Kids then only change the questions, title and colours by prompting.
- **The code is too long or gets cut off.** Keep the recipe's "one file, no internet files" rule, and ask "continue" if needed. Test the recipe on every tool in the test run.
- **Safety.** The game runs inside the chatbot's preview or as a local file. The no-internet-links rule keeps it self-contained and safe to run offline on Day 5.
